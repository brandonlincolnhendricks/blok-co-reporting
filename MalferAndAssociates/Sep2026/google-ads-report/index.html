  • Where clicks came from: 523 of 680 clicks (77%) came from people in the Kansas City area and 157 from elsewhere. The campaign is set to show in Kansas and Missouri, and also in New York City, Seattle and Los Angeles County.

What this means for you

More of the people who see your ad are choosing to click it, at a steady price per click. The bigger opening is ad rank, which sharper ad wording can help with over time; we are not recommending a bigger budget.

BANNER ADS

Banner ads - your name reached about 10,200 people for $243

Banners keep your name in front of people as they read and browse online, so we judge them on being seen, not on leads. Shown here is your most-shown banner, the 970x250 size, with 3,434 views and 11 clicks.

Shown: 3 of the 12 sizes of this design. Across all 12 sizes this month: 15,596 views, 94 clicks.
  • Reach: your banners were shown 15,653 times to about 10,200 people, about 1.5 times each. Of the views Google could measure, 65% (10,016 of 15,495) were on screen, against 63% in a usual month.
  • Cost: every 1,000 views cost $15.55, against $12.28 last month and $11.77 in a usual month, so the same $243 reached about 10,200 people instead of about 13,900 last month.
  • Clicks: 95, against 187 last month and 218 in a usual month. 73 of the 95 came from phones, so we treat banner clicks as a light signal.

What this means for you

Think of a banner as a billboard: judge it by how many people it reaches and whether it is seen. Leads are a bonus. This month each view cost more, and the next section shows where the views landed.

Plain-English glossary
Reach
How many different people were shown your banners, counting each person once. It is Google's estimate, so treat it as approximate.
Views on screen
A view is each time a banner was shown on a page. It counts as on screen when a large part of the ad was visible long enough to be seen, by Google's standard.
Cost per 1,000 views
What it cost to show your banners 1,000 times.
A usual month
The average of the months June to August, before this one. It keeps an unusually busy or quiet month from setting the bar.
Lead
An ad visitor who tapped your phone number on your website, signed up on your home search, or sent a form such as a contact, showing or home-value request. A phone tap shows the tap, not whether a call followed. Messages through Local Services ads are counted separately.
Home-search sign-up
A visitor created an account on the listings search on your website, usually to save homes or get new-listing alerts. An early buyer signal, not a request to talk.

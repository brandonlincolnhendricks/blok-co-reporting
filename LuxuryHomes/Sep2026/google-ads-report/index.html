YOUR LEADS

Leads - 8 this month, up from 4 last month

A lead is an ad visitor who reached out or signed up: a home-search sign-up, a tap on your phone number, a call from your ad's call button, or a form such as a contact or showing request. You had 8, against 4 last month and 4 to 5 in a usual month, and all of them came from your Williamson County Home Search ads.

What this means for you

Leads rose while clicks held steady, so more of your ad visitors took a step toward you. With counts this small a month can swing either way, so we read it as a good sign rather than a new normal.

COST PER LEAD

You spent $999, close to last month's $1,011 and a usual month's $1,006. With more leads for about the same spend, each lead cost $125, against $253 last month and $215 in a usual month.

What this means for you

The same budget brought more leads this month. We are not recommending any budget change; our focus is on what the ads say and which searches they reach.

ROOM TO GROW

Room to grow - your ads showed on 15% of the searches they qualified for

Your ads showed on 15% of the searches they qualified for this month, against 14% last month and 16% across June to August. They missed 66% because they ranked too low and 18% because the day's budget ran out. Google sets ad rank from the bid and from how useful the ad and its page look.

  • Missed for rank: 66%, against 68% last month and 66% across June to August.
  • Missed for budget: 18%, the same as last month's 18%.
  • Your ads appeared 8,362 times, up from 8,064 last month and 6,841 in a usual month.
  • Of those appearances, 5.5% led to a click, against 5.6% last month and 6.6% in a usual month.

What this means for you

Rank, more than budget, is what keeps these ads off most searches. Sharper ad wording and pages that match each search are the next things to improve, though we cannot promise a ranking change.

What we recommend next

Recommendations for the coming month

1. Test new ad wording for home searches

Ad wording test

We recommend testing new ad wording built around what people searched for, such as homes for sale in Franklin and Brentwood, and checking that each ad opens a page that matches its search, all within the current budget. The aim is to win more of the searches the ads now miss because they rank too low. We will report the share of searches shown each month, though we cannot promise a ranking change.

2. Screen out searches for other firms and for rentals

Search words

We recommend screening out searches for other real estate firms by name and searches for rentals, a few at a time, and watching leads as we go. Of the searches Google shows, these brought clicks but no leads this month. Searches for homes in Nashville, Franklin and Brentwood stay in.

3

Targeting

We recommend trimming the campaign's location settings to the areas you want, once you confirm them. It is set to show in places such as Los Angeles and New York City as well as Williamson, Davidson and Wilson counties, while clicks come mainly from the Nashville area. We will not change anything without your go-ahead.

4

4. Keep an eye on calls from the ad's call button

Keep watching

We recommend no change yet and keeping an eye on calls from your ad's call button, last recorded on September 8. They have had a longer quiet stretch before, from June 2 to August 17, so this is not unusual yet. If they stay quiet through the coming month, we will suggest a quick test call.

Plain-English glossary
Lead
An ad visitor who signed up on your home search, tapped your phone number on your website, called from the ad's call button, or sent a form such as a contact or showing request. A phone tap shows the tap, not whether a call followed.
Home-search sign-up
A visitor created an account on the listings search on your website, usually to save homes or get new-listing alerts. An early buyer signal, not a request to talk.
Cost per lead
Your ad spend for the month divided by the leads that came in. It swings a lot when lead counts are small.
Click rate
The share of times your ad appeared on Google that ended in a click.
Share of searches shown
Out of all the searches your ads qualified for, the share where your ad actually appeared. The rest were missed because the ad ranked too low or the day's budget ran out.
A usual month
The average of June to August 2026, the months before this one. It keeps an unusually busy or quiet month from setting the bar.

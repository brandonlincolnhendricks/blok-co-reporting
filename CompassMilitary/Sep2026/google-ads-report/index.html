BANNER ADS

Banner ads - your name reached about 66,400 people for $242

Banners keep your name in front of people as they read and browse online, so we judge them on being seen, not on leads. In September your banners were shown 124,489 times to about 66,400 people, about 1.9 times each.

Shown: 3 of the 12 sizes of this design. Across all 12 sizes this month: 70,368 views, 121 clicks.
  • On screen: 88,133 views were actually on screen, 71% of the views Google could measure, against 70% in August and 65% in a usual month.
  • Cost: every 1,000 views cost $1.95, against $3.86 in August and $3.67 in a usual month, so spend of $242, close to August's $243, reached about 66,400 people instead of about 27,900. That is about $4 per 1,000 people reached, down from about $9.
  • Clicks: 264, up from 215 in August and 221 in a usual month, at $0.92 each on average against $1.13. The click rate was 0.2%, against 0.3% in August, and 130 of the 264 clicks came from phones, so we treat clicks as a light signal.
  • Design: the banner shown most was Proud To Serve in its 970x250 size, with 59,606 views and 98 clicks.

What this means for you

Think of a banner as a billboard: judge it by how many people it reaches and whether it is actually seen. In September more people saw your name, each view cost less and a similar share was on screen.

BANNER PLACEMENTS

Where banners ran - Fox News carried 100,895 of 124,489 views

Your banner campaign has a chosen list of websites, and 56 of the 248 sites on that list showed your banners in September. Fox News carried most of the views, and the five sites with the most views together carried 90% of them.

  • After Fox News came buzzfeed.com (4,024 views), thepioneerwoman.com (2,647), eonline.com (2,324) and Business Insider (1,958).
  • The real estate site and the 3 design sites on your list showed no banners, and the 4 lifestyle sites carried 1,203 views, about 1% of the total.
  • Costs varied by site: Business Insider charged $10.30 per 1,000 views, while buzzfeed.com charged $1.75.
  • Google does not tie 2,607 views (2.1%) to a named website, so the site list does not add up to every view.

What this means for you

The opportunity is the real estate, design and lifestyle sites already on your list, which carried few or none of your views. We will look into why before we go through the list with you.

BANNER AREAS

Where viewers were - across the United States, with 553 views in San Diego County

Your banner campaign is set to show anywhere in the United States, so views were spread across the country. Among the views Google could tie to a location, San Diego County had 553, about 0.5%, with 1 click.

  • The towns with the most views were Dallas (2,445), Chicago (2,186), Phoenix (1,729), Indianapolis (1,715), Los Angeles (1,661) and Atlanta (1,595).
  • The counties with the most views were Dallas County (3,612), Maricopa County (2,872) and Cook County (2,743).
  • Devices: 72.2% of views were on desktop computers, while clicks were split between phones (130) and desktops (127).

What this means for you

If your team mainly works in San Diego, most of this reach is far from your market. If you also help people moving to San Diego from elsewhere, some wider reach may be useful, and your answer shapes our next step on where the banners show.

LEADS

Leads - none this month, the same as August

A lead is an ad visitor who reached out: a tap on your phone number, a contact or consultation request, a showing request or another form your account counts. None came in from your ads in September or in August. Since June 1, your banners have brought one contact request, on June 4.

No leads came in from the ads this month.
  • A usual month: 0 to 1 lead, because that June request is spread across June to August.
  • Later visits: Google also counts people who saw a banner without clicking and reached out later on your website. That was 0 this month, as in August.
  • Contact requests have been quiet since June 4. A quick test request will show whether new requests are being counted.

What this means for you

Banners are judged on being seen, so a lead from them is a bonus, not their scorecard. The test request simply makes sure that any request that does come in is counted.

Plain-English glossary
Reach
How many different people were shown your banners, counting each person once. It is Google's estimate, so treat it as approximate, and it cannot be added across months.
Views on screen
A view is a time a banner was shown on a page. It counts as on screen when enough of the ad was visible, for long enough, to meet Google's on-screen standard.
Cost per 1,000 views
What it cost to show your banners 1,000 times.
Click rate
Out of all the times a banner was shown, the share that led to a click.
Lead
An ad visitor who tapped your phone number on your website or sent a form your account counts, such as a contact, consultation or showing request. Taps on your Google listing are not counted as leads.
A usual month
The average of the months from June to August. It keeps a busy or quiet month from setting the bar on its own.

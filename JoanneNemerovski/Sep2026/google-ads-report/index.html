BANNER ADS

Banner ads - your name reached about 70,700 people for $241

Banners keep your name in front of people as they read the news, follow sports or browse on their phones, so we judge them on being seen, not on leads. In September your banners were shown 104,161 times to about 70,700 people, about 1.5 times each.

  • Reach: about 70,700 people, up from about 48,000 in August and about 58,600 in a usual month. Each month is measured on its own, so these figures cannot be added together.
  • Cost: $2.31 per 1,000 views, down from $3.16 in August and close to the usual $2.26. That works out to about $3 per 1,000 people reached, against about $5 last month.
  • On screen: 75% of the views Google could measure were actually on screen (77,550 of 104,057), up from 73% in August and 69% in a usual month.
  • Spend: $241, about the same as August's $243 and below a usual month's $323.

What this means for you

Think of a banner as a billboard on the drive into the city: judge it by how many people see it and whether it is actually on screen. This month it reached more people for about the same money as August.

BANNER DESIGNS

Banner designs - each design drew 167 clicks

Your banners rotate between designs, and Google showed them about equally. Real Estate Is Personal was shown 50,624 times and the other design 53,537 times, and each drew 167 clicks. The banner shown most was Real Estate Is Personal in the 336x280 size, with 12,770 views.

Shown: 3 of the 12 sizes of this design. Across all 12 sizes this month: 50,624 views, 167 clicks.
  • Clicks: 334 in all, up from 201 in August, against 377 in a usual month. The click rate held at about 3 per 1,000 views (0.3%).
  • Cost per click: $0.72, down from $1.21 in August and $0.85 in a usual month.
  • Phones: 81.0% of views were on mobile, and 286 of 334 banner clicks came from phones. Taps on a small screen can include accidental ones, so we treat banner clicks as a light signal.

What this means for you

Both designs are being seen about equally, so there is no need to change them yet. Clicks are a bonus, not the scorecard.

BANNER LOCATIONS

Where viewers were - 63.4% of placed views in Cook County

The campaign is set to show in Chicago. Some views had no location, so these shares count only the views Google could place. Of those, 63.4% (61,236 views) were in Cook County, led by Chicago itself with 55,919, then Norridge (485), Park Ridge (356) and Lincolnwood (350).

  • Cook County viewers gave 174 of the 334 banner clicks.
  • Nearby, DuPage County had 1,790 views and Will County 1,099.
  • Farther away, views showed up in Minneapolis (3,440), Indianapolis (2,557), Birmingham (2,479), Milwaukee (842) and Dallas (695), with small numbers in other states and abroad.

What this means for you

Views in Minneapolis or Dallas do little to keep your name in front of buyers and sellers in Chicago. Checking the location setting could keep more of the same budget in your home market.

Plain-English glossary
Reach
How many different people were shown your banners, counting each person once. It is Google's estimate, so treat it as approximate, and each month is measured on its own.
Views on screen
A view is a time a banner was shown on a page. It counts as on screen when it met Google's standard for being visible; a banner low on a page nobody scrolls to does not count.
Cost per 1,000 views
What it cost to show your banners a thousand times. Lower means more views for the same budget.
Click rate
Out of every 1,000 times a banner was shown, how many times someone clicked it.
Lead
Someone who, after clicking an ad, tapped your phone number, signed up on your home search, or sent a request such as a contact, showing or home-value request.
A usual month
The average of June to August, the months before this one. It keeps an unusually busy or quiet month from setting the bar.

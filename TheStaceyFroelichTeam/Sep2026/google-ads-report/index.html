BANNER ADS

Banner ads - your name reached about 21,000 people for $226

Banners keep your name in front of people as they read the news and follow sports, so we judge them on being seen, not on leads. This month they were shown 58,334 times to about 21,000 people, about 2.8 times each, against about 10,800 people at about 3.8 times each last month.

  • Cost: every 1,000 views cost $3.87, down from $5.99 last month, and putting your name in front of 1,000 different people cost about $11, against about $23.
  • Spend: $226 this month against $244 in August, with banner views up from 40,706 to 58,334.
  • On screen: 71% (41,603 of 58,306 measured views) were actually on screen, against 79% (32,293 of 40,694 measured views) last month.
  • Clicks: 412, up from 387, at a click rate of 0.7% against 1.0% last month. Of these, 264 of 412 banner clicks came from phones, so we treat banner clicks as a light signal.

What this means for you

Think of a banner as a billboard on the avenue: judge it by how many people it reaches and whether it is actually seen. This month more people saw your name for less money, each a little less often, and a smaller share of views were on screen.

LEADS

Leads - 0 this month, as in August

A lead is an ad visitor who reached out or signed up, for example a tap on your phone number, a home-search sign-up, or a contact, showing or home-value request. Leads were 0 this month and 0 last month, and your banner campaign has recorded none since June 1.

No leads came in from the ads this month.
  • Your account counts phone taps, calls from ads, home-search sign-ups and requests for contact, showings, consultations, appointments, home values, property details and market reports.
  • Google also counted 0 view-through leads this month, meaning no one who saw a banner without clicking later signed up or reached out, so the banners are not credited with any later leads.

What this means for you

Banners are a billboard, so any lead from them is a bonus, not their scorecard. As nothing has been recorded since June 1, a quick test sign-up will confirm a lead is counted when a lead does come.

Plain-English glossary
Reach
How many different people were shown your banners, counting each person once. It is Google's estimate, so treat it as approximate, and it cannot be added across months.
Views on screen
A view is a time a banner was shown on a page. It counts as on screen when at least half the ad was visible for at least a second.
Cost per 1,000 views
What it cost to show your banners 1,000 times.
Click rate
The share of banner views that led to a click.
Lead
An ad visitor who tapped your phone number, signed up on your home search, or sent a request such as a contact, showing or home-value request. A phone tap shows the tap, not whether a call followed.
View-through lead
Someone who saw a banner without clicking and later signed up or reached out on your website.
